\section{SysML Diagram}
We have used different SysML diagrams to visually represent the system's structure and behavior.
\subsection{Activity Diagram}
This diagram models the runtime behavior of a single vehicle as it interacts with the intersection controller:
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{C:/Users/exone/Documents/GitHub/Embedded_Engineering_Lab/Diagrams/Activity_diagram.png}
    \caption{Activity diagram}
    \label{fig:Activity diagram}
\end{figure} 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Receive arrival + turn intent
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
            \item A vehicle arrives at the stop line and communicates its intended movement (straight, left, or right).
        \end{enumerate}
    \item Determine bundle
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
            \item Based on direction (North South or East West) and turn intent (through or left), the vehicle is assigned to one of four “bundles”: NS-TR, EW-TR, NS-L, or EW-L.
        \end{enumerate}  
    \item Enqueue into bundle queue
            \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
            \item The controller places the request in the corresponding queue for later scheduling.
            \end{enumerate}  
    \item Check against current green phase
            \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
                \item If the vehicle bundle matches the currently active (green) phase, it bypasses waiting; otherwise:
                \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
                \item Send “WAIT” signal
                \item Remain queued until the green phase switches back.
                \end{enumerate}  
            \end{enumerate}  
    \item Send “GO” signal
            \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
                \item When its bundle is selected by the scheduling algorithm, the controller grants green.
            \end{enumerate}  
    \item Cross intersection safely
            \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
                \item The vehicle proceeds through the junction under the granted green signal.
            \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Requirement Diagram}
This UML requirements diagram organizes and decomposes the system-level requirement “Intersection Control” into five constituent requirements:
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{C:/Users/exone/Documents/GitHub/Embedded_Engineering_Lab/Diagrams/requirement_diagram.jpg}
    \caption{Requirement diagram}
    \label{fig:Requirement diagram}
\end{figure} 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Vehicle Detection
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
            \item``The system shall detect and count vehicles approaching from all directions''.
        \end{enumerate}
    \item Movement Schedule
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
            \item``The system shall schedule non-conflicting movements to maximize parallel throughput''.
        \end{enumerate}  
    \item Central Hub Communication
            \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
            \item ``The system shall send queue information to and receive grants from a central controller''.
            \end{enumerate}  
    \item Parameter Configuration
            \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
                \item ``The system shall allow configuration of timing parameters (e.g., green/gap duration)''.
            \end{enumerate}  
    \item Safety Enforcement
            \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
                \item ``The system shall enforce all-red gaps and prevent any conflicting movements''.
            \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
